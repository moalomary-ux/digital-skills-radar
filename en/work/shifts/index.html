How government work is changing

The government work-shift map

How the tasks of 8 government job families change between 2026 and 2030: what gets automated, what gets augmented, what is new and what stays human. For each family: the rising skills, and what a manager can do now.

Qualitative bands only, no automation percentages.Editorial estimate

Policy analyst

Moves from gathering and summarising information to testing options with data and weighing their consequences for beneficiaries.

A signal from a published source: The OECD Digital Government Policy Framework names a “data-driven public sector” as one of 6 dimensions of a digital government.

How the tasks change, 2026–2030

Automated

Routine tasks systems are likely to take over, with human review.

  • Compiling references and international practice into a first summary
  • Summarising long studies and reports
  • Drafting first-pass comparison tables across countries and entities

Augmented

Tasks where the employee stays responsible and tools speed the work.

  • Analysing data to support policy options
  • Drafting and revising policy papers
  • Tracking public response to proposals
  • Checking a proposal against existing regulations

New

New tasks the transformation itself creates.

  • Checking generative AI output before it enters a policy paper
  • Designing small pilots to test a policy before scaling it
  • Assessing algorithms that support public decisions

Stays human

Tasks built on judgment, relationships and accountability.

  • Balancing competing interests and proposing a compromise
  • Consulting stakeholders and building consensus
  • Judging what is fair and socially acceptable

Rising skills

  • Reading data and its limitsAI & Data
  • Digital legislation awarenessPolicy & Governance
  • Storytelling with dataLeadership

Related digital roles

  • Digital Policy Advisor
  • AI Ethics Officer
  • Performance Analyst

What a manager can do now

  1. 1Require every policy paper to cite the source of each figure and who checked it.
  2. 2Give analysts time to practise data analysis with the entity’s own tools.
  3. 3Pilot one policy this year at small scale before rolling it out.

How to read this map

  • Placing tasks in the four lanes is an editorial reading of published trends, not a measurement of any entity or job.
  • Matching government job families to the international classification of occupations is approximate, and tasks differ from one entity to another.
  • “Automated” does not mean the job disappears; it means a specific task moves to systems while human review remains.
