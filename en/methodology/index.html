What this assessment is, and is not

The assessment in this tool is a self-assessment that helps you explore your development priorities across the eight domains. It is a starting point for a development conversation, not a measurement of your ability.

What it is

  • A self-rating of your skills, complemented by professional-judgment scenarios and knowledge-check questions that help you review it.
  • A tool for self-development and for a conversation with your manager about a development plan of 90 days.
  • Its results stay in your browser and are sent to no one.

What it is not

  • Not a measurement of your ability or an exam, and it confers no certificate or certified level.
  • Not for hiring, promotion, selection, performance appraisal or any decision about an employee.
  • It ranks no one and gives no overall score.

The scale: four levels

You rate each skill on a 0–4 scale, and a domain score is the mean of its skill ratings. The score converts to one of 4 levels at the thresholds 1.25 · 2.25 · 3.25.

The thresholds are declared editorial choices not derived from data, and the scale has not been statistically validated. A level helps you read your self-rating; it does not describe your actual performance.

Professional-judgment scenarios and the knowledge check

At the start you choose your role category from 5 categories:

  • General
  • Applied
  • Specialist
  • Team leadership
  • Executive

The scenario bank holds 40 scenarios, one per domain in your role category. Each has four options scored 0–3 by the maturity of the response, and the rationale appears after you answer.

The knowledge check holds 40 questions on foundational knowledge in each domain. It keeps how many you answered and how many were correct, and consistency with your self-rating is not judged before 3 questions in the domain.

The scenarios and knowledge-check questions are editorial: written and edited by the tool’s author, not field-tested with pilot entities and not statistically validated.

How are development priorities derived?

One priority engine reads four inputs and suggests development priorities. The results page, the plan and the printed report all read from it, so the priority never differs from one page to another.

Reasons for a priority

  • Your self-rating is below the indicative target for your role category.
  • Your scenario responses are below your self-rating.
  • Both your self-rating and your scenario responses are below the target.
  • A high self-rating that neither the scenarios nor the knowledge check support, so checking it in practice is suggested.

If you choose a specific role from the role library (24 roles), its indicative target replaces the category target.

No overall score, no general “readiness” and no ranking of people. The target is an indicative editorial one for the role category, not a job standard. A suggested priority is an invitation to develop, not a measurement of a shortfall in your ability.

Technologies changing government work

A list of 48 technologies, each linked to the skills it requires. The aim is to show which skills to prepare for, not to rate the technologies themselves.

  • Horizon: 0–2 · 2–5 · 5–10 years. It is an editorial judgment of when a technology’s use in government work broadens.
  • The technology readiness scale TRL 1–9 describes a technology’s global maturity, not its adoption in Saudi Arabia, and appears only in the details with an “editorial” badge.
  • Each judgment is a desk review against named published signals, with a written rationale.
  • The list is an independent reading, not an official radar of any organization.

Government-entity data and its source

Entity figures come from two source workbooks: the enablement service records (training and consulting) and the entity and sector directory. Both are internal records of the government digital-skills program and the consulting and studies platform, up to the end of the third quarter of 2026. The two units differ, training participation records (not unique people) and consulting services, so they are never added into one figure.

The tool shows the sheet totals because they are consistent with the sector aggregates, and states the other two figures with their dates. The owner decides the confirmed figure, and the tool is updated then.

  • The published build shows the detailed layer by the owner’s decision: national figures, aggregates for sectors with n ≥ 10 or more entities with records, each government entity’s service record as it appears in the two source workbooks, and entity logos from the official directory.
  • No ranking of entities, no labelling of an entity as not served, no logo of the Authority itself and no claim of official status; the records inform development planning and never rate an entity.
  • An aggregates-only build (no records, no logos) remains available through a single build flag when needed.
  • “Government entity” means government entities only; other types (owned companies, non-profits, clubs) are named by their type.

Mapping consulting categories to domains

The consulting categories in the service records are used to suggest “development domains linked to the consulting record” at sector level. This is an editorial inference: an entity requesting a consultation does not measure its staff’s needs, and the weights below are declared assumptions, not measurements.

Privacy

  • Nothing leaves your browser: your answers and results are stored on your device only, and you can delete them at any time.
  • No accounts, no analytics or tracking tools, and no advertising cookies.
  • Fonts are hosted with the site itself, so the page sends no request to any third party on load.
  • If you choose to copy your result code, it carries only rounded scores and your role category, with no name or personal data, and it is never sent to a server.
  • No link to an employee file or to any HR system.

Independence statement

The Digital Skills Radar is an independent concept, not an official product of any organization. Its author prepared it in a personal capacity; it represents no official position of any government entity and uses no official logos. Citing frameworks, reports and indices does not mean their publishers endorse this tool.

Known limitations

  • The scale, scenarios and knowledge-check questions have not yet been statistically validated.
  • Thresholds, weights and indicative targets are editorial.
  • Entity data stops at the end of the third quarter of 2026, and training records are participation records, not people.
  • The Arabic text is the reference; the English translation is editorial.
  • Some sources on the standards page have not yet been matched to their original text, and are marked medium or low confidence.

Update cadence

  • A half-yearly review of the technology list and its horizons.
  • A quarterly review of fast-moving national figures and entity data.
  • A yearly review of the role library, its indicative targets and the scenario bank.
  • Every claim carries its last review date, and the evidence register flags it as needing review once 12 months have passed.
